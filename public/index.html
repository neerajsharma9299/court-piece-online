        ♠ Spades

        Trump is always spades

        How to Play Spades

        Spades is a 4-player trick-taking game. Spades are always trump -- there's no dealer's choice like in Court Piece.

        Bidding

        Before play, every player looks at their 13 cards and bids how many tricks they think they'll win, from 0 up to 13. A bid of 0 is called Nil -- a bet that you'll win zero tricks, worth a big bonus if you pull it off and a big penalty if you don't.

        Team vs Individual

        In Team mode you and the player across from you share a combined bid and score together, just like Court Piece's teams. In Individual mode all four players bid and score separately -- every player for themselves.

        Playing a Trick

        Follow the suit that was led if you can. Highest card of the led suit wins the trick -- unless a spade is played, which beats everything. You can't lead a spade until spades have been "broken" (played on an earlier trick because a player had no cards of the suit that was led), unless spades are all you have left in hand.

        Scoring

        Make your bid (or your team's combined bid) and you score 10 points per trick bid, plus 1 point for every trick you win beyond your bid ("bags"). Fall short of your bid and you lose 10 points per trick bid instead. A successful Nil is worth +100; a failed Nil costs -100. Every 10 bags accumulated costs a 100-point penalty. First to the target score wins.

        How to Play

        Court Piece is a 4-player trick-taking game, played in two fixed teams: you and the player across from you versus the other two.

        Trump & Challenge

        Each round, the dealer's left-hand player picks the trump suit. Going around the table, every other player then gets one chance to change the trump instead -- the dealer gets the final say. If someone changes it, their team must win all 13 tricks to score; if the other team wins two tricks in a row, the challenge fails instantly.

        Tricks & Capturing

        Follow the suit that was led if you can. Highest card of the led suit wins the trick -- unless someone plays trump, which beats everything. Tricks don't score immediately: they pile up until one player wins two in a row, at which point they claim the whole pile. The 13th trick always goes to whoever wins it, streak or not.

        Winning a Round

        In a normal round, the trump-holding team needs 8 of the 13 tricks. Win all 13 and it's a "court" -- an automatic point. First team to 5 round points wins the match.
